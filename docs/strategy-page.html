Question 1 of 4

Rules differ by state and city. Does the problem change by region?

Yes. Regulation does not remove the problem, it moves it. Hidden fees hurt most where there is no law; buildings find it cheapest to self-report where there is one.

Texas has no all-in price rule, but Austin passed its own itemised fee-list ordinance on May 28, 2026 (City of Austin). Colorado, Massachusetts, Seattle and others have their own rules, and the FTC opened a rulemaking in March 2026. So we store rules as data in a jurisdictions table and show a "Your rights here" box on each building page. A new city is a new row, not new code.

Question 2 of 4

Manual data won't scale. How do buildings self-report, and how does the flywheel start?

Buildings report because it saves them work and brings them renters who will pass screening. Renter bills check that they told the truth.

  1. Comply once, publish everywhere. A free form produces the fee list a city or state now requires (and, in Texas, the §92.3515 selection criteria), and publishes an honest listing.
  2. Data is the price of a lead. Only self-reporting buildings receive approval-ready leads: renters whose income range, ID type and guarantor plans already match the building's rules.
  3. Silence is visible. Self-reporters get a "Verified by building" badge. Others show "Not reported by the building" beside our estimate.

This is give-to-get, the mechanism Glassdoor uses for reviews and salaries (Glassdoor), applied to buildings. Renters never pay for the core search.

Flywheel target, spring 2027 Goal
Buildings with data near UTD 30
Share that self-report 30%
Median age of a building's data < 30 days
Move-ins who share one bill 1 in 4
Self-report vs renter-bill mismatch < 10%

Kill signal: if fewer than 2 operators self-report after seeing a lead report, data stays manual and Honest Rent stays a small local guide.

Question 3 of 4

Competitors can copy the features. Feature or company?

Real monthly cost alone is a feature, and new laws push listing sites to copy it. The company is the newcomer's on-ramp to US renting.

  • Approval data no one else collects: rules for renters without an SSN or US credit now, anonymous outcomes later. Each outcome improves the next prediction.
  • Renter Passport: apply once with verified income, an international credit report and a guarantor quote, through a partner reporting agency. Colorado already makes landlords accept portable reports.
  • First-year money help: guarantor and deposit options, move-in cash, and rent reporting to build US credit through a partner. An Urban Institute trial found rent reporting raised the share of renters with a score by 12 points (Bankrate).
  • Counter-positioning: we never rank by who pays. Listing sites earn from paid placement, so copying that promise would cost them revenue.

Question 4 of 4

The design looked generated. What changed, and why?

The new look borrows from two documents every newcomer handles: the itemised receipt and the passport stamp.

  • Search first, like real rental sites, instead of a marketing hero. The home page opens on the search and the listings.
  • The same four cost columns on every card (Rent · Fees · Utilities · Move-in), borrowed from Japanese rental listings, with "Not disclosed" wherever a building hasn't shared a figure (like the UK rule that rent and deposit must be filled in). One big total leads, Airbnb-style, and the full itemised breakdown opens on tap.
  • Map thumbnails of each building's block instead of drawn covers: real, useful, and copied from nowhere.
  • One highlighter colour, used only on charges the advert hid, the way renters mark up their own ledgers. If it is yellow, it was hidden.
  • Approval as an ink stamp (Passport OK, Guarantor needed, Unlikely), because approval is a decision someone makes about you, and stamps are how newcomers meet that decision.
  • Ballpoint blue for actions instead of the default teal: the pen you sign a lease with.

We also checked the result against published lists of "AI design tells" and removed what matched: the pill above the headline, a highlighted headline phrase, capital monospace labels, icon-tile step cards and blurred colour bands. The full reasons for each choice are in the repository's DESIGN.md.
